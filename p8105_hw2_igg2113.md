The subway data contain one row for each station entrance/exit. I kept
the necessary variables (line, station name, station latitude/longitude,
route columns, entry, vending, entrance type, ADA compliance). I changed
‘entry’ from yes/no to true/false. The dataset has 1868 rows and 19
columns. The data are not tidy because the route served is in eleven
columns instead of one variable. There are 465 stations (line and
station name). Of them, 84 are ADA compliant. 37.7 percent of entrances
and exits without vending allow entry. When I changed route columns into
a route number and a route name, I determined 60 distinct stations serve
the A train and 17 of them are ADA compliant.

Starting with the baseline data, I skipped the first descriptive row on
import, treated `.` as missing, and recoded sex and APOE4 carrier status
as text rather than numeric values. I removed 4 participants who already
had MCI at baseline. 483 participants were recruited, and 479 met the
inclusion criteria. Of them, 93 developed MCI. The average baseline age
is 65.03 years. 30% of women are APOE4 carriers. I also skipped the
descriptive first row for the longitudinal biomarker data and changed
the visit columns into one long time variable. This resulted in 2435
rows and 3 columns (`id`, `years_since_baseline`, `amyloid_ratio`).
Eight participants appear only in the baseline data; 16 appear only in
the biomarker data. Combining the two datasets and keeping only
participants in both gives 2355 rows and 8 columns. I saved this as
`data/mci_combined.csv`.
